Scope note: this file describes the product this repo is designing for. It is not product truth for the repo's own tooling. Research, decisions and stakeholder inputs live in the project notes. The visual world lives in a surface's design record, not here.

Two rules govern the fill, and they hold on both routes:

- **Omit a section that does not apply**, rather than filling it with generic prose. An empty heading is a heading the next reader trusts.
- **Label an inferred fact as inferred.** Write "inferred from the brief" or "assumed, unconfirmed" beside anything nobody stated.

## Platform

Web, native iOS, native Android, or the combination. One line.

## Users

One entry per role. For each: what they have, what they are trying to do, and the situation they are in when they open the product. Name the mechanism that separates the roles, where one exists.

## Product Purpose

What the product does, in the stakeholders' own words where they have given them. Two or three sentences.

## Positioning

Who the product is measured against, and the one axis on which it has to win. Name the incumbent.

## Operating Context

Where and how the product gets used: the device, the network, the lighting, the interruption, the hands. A constraint that comes from the context rather than from the code belongs here.

## Capabilities and Constraints

What the product must be able to do, and what it cannot do. Regulatory limits, integration dependencies, data residency, offline behaviour, and anything the stakeholders have ruled out.

## Brand Commitments

The commitments the identity makes, in words rather than hex values. The theme file carries the values; this section carries what they promise. Where a brand book exists, name it.

Where no brand exists yet, write **Pending** here along with what it waits on, who owns it, and when it is expected. Surfaces stay unbranded until it lands, which is a decision rather than a gap, and this is the only place that decision is written down.

## Evidence on Hand

What this file is built from. One line per source: a document, an interview, a tracker project, a transcript in the project notes. Mark anything that is an assumption instead of a source.

## Product Principles

Three to six principles that decide a close call. Each must be able to reject a plausible design. A principle nothing could fail is a slogan.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The bar this product holds itself to, and any population it must serve that the default bar misses. Name the contrast standard, the target size, the input methods, and the languages.
